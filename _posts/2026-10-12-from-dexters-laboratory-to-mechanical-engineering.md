I was always curious about how appliances and machines worked. That interest led me from HVAC and appliance service in Vadodara, Gujarat, to mechanical design work in Canada.

## The kid who wanted to know how things worked

Before I knew engineering drawings, CAD, or thermodynamics, I wondered what happened inside everyday machines. Why did a motor spin? How did an air conditioner cool a room? What made an appliance stop working?

One of the shows I remember is *Dexter’s Laboratory*. I liked watching a curious character experiment, build things, and work through problems. I did not know it then, but that kind of curiosity would stay with me.

## From curiosity to hands-on service

As I got older, I became interested in mechanical engineering, electronics, and the practical side of understanding equipment. I wanted to know how components worked together, what could cause a failure, and how to investigate it step by step.

That interest led me to HVAC and appliance service in Vadodara, Gujarat, where I became involved in Shreeji Enterprises and its customer-service operations.

## Service work: technical problems and people

Most customers came with a symptom, not a technical diagnosis. The work was to understand the complaint, investigate the equipment, identify possible causes, and agree on the next step.

Service work also depended on good coordination. Customer visits had to be arranged, expectations explained, spare parts organized, and time managed. Customers needed to understand what work was being done and why. I learned that a useful repair had to be safe, practical, and clear to the person relying on it.

Working with HVAC and appliance systems made me more interested in how motors, electrical controls, airflow, and heat interact. I also learned to look beyond the first fault: what else might contribute to it, whether the repair could be accessed, and how the equipment could be maintained.

## From field service to mechanical design

Today, I work as a Mechanical Designer at Power Bus Way, a Legrand company, in Mississauga, Ontario. My work includes 3D CAD models, engineering drawings, mechanical assemblies, mounting interfaces, and coordination with manufacturing teams.

Power-distribution equipment is different from HVAC systems, but I still use the same habits: understand how the parts work together, check the constraints, and look for a practical solution. My service background also reminds me to consider the people who will assemble, install, and maintain the equipment.

## What I want to keep learning

I want to keep developing across mechanical design, HVAC equipment, product development, and electromechanical systems. The starting point has not changed much since I was a kid: understand how the equipment works before deciding how to improve it.
